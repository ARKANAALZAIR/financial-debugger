# Evidence Audit

## Evidence quality dimensions

Score qualitatively, not numerically unless the underlying methodology supports it:

1. Relevance — does the evidence bear directly on the claim?
2. Reliability — is the source reasonably authoritative for this fact?
3. Recency — is the information current enough for the question?
4. Scope — does it cover the same entity/period/population?
5. Directness — does it test the claim itself or only a nearby proposition?
6. Independence — are multiple sources actually independent?
7. Reproducibility — can the result be checked?

## Evidence hierarchy

Preferred when available:
- company filings and official disclosures
- exchange/regulator data
- audited financial statements
- central-bank/government statistics
- official investor materials

Useful secondary sources:
- established financial media
- reputable research
- industry analysis

Weak signals requiring caution:
- anonymous posts
- screenshots without provenance
- social posts without primary sources
- reposts and summaries that remove context

Source hierarchy is not proof. A strong source can still have limited scope or contain a claim that does not support the user's conclusion.

## Evidence-to-claim alignment

For every important claim:

`Claim → exact supporting evidence → exact limitation`

If the source only supports a weaker proposition, say so.

Example:
- Source: “management expects strong demand.”
- User claim: “revenue will grow 30%.”
- Result: source does not support the numerical forecast.

## Evidence sufficiency

Ask whether the strength and amount of evidence are appropriate for the strength of the conclusion.

One positive quarter may support “improved recently,” but generally not “structurally superior for the next five years.”


## Evidence sufficiency

Evidence sufficiency asks whether the strength, directness, scope, and quantity of evidence justify the strength of the conclusion.

A source can be credible yet insufficient for a stronger claim. A source can also be primary yet limited in scope. The debugger should preserve those distinctions.

## Evidence Map

For material claims, track:
`Claim | Supporting | Contradicting | Missing | Source quality | Alignment | Sufficiency | Decision impact`
